# How Molecular Weight Shapes the Relative Importance of Chemical and Structural Descriptors for Aqueous Solubility

## Abstract

<jats:p>Learning aqueous solubility remains a key challenge in drug development for improving oral bioavailability. Traditional data-driven solubility estimations using standard supervised models, however, can often suppress the information embedded in a candidate molecule's chemical and structural properties. In addition to the predictive analyses for solubility, it is also important to investigate the underlying inference associated with the solubility prediction of a potential drug candidate. This paper attempts to learn such properties and further proposes an analytic framework with an additive characterization that provides insights into the differential contributions of chemical and graphical descriptors. The dataset used in this work includes over 9000 molecules with structural, chemical, and electronic properties. Using graph-theoretic attributes derived from molecular graphs, we characterize the structural traits of the molecules associated with aqueous solubility. Our analyses highlight that the relative importance of chemical and structural descriptors shifts systematically with molecular weight: chemical descriptors dominate for molecules below the median weight, whereas graph-theoretic descriptors gain importance above it, reflecting the increased importance of atomic connectivity in heavier molecules.</jats:p>
