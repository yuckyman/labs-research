# Effects of LPG Shortage in Financial Market

## Abstract

<jats:p>The Strait of Hormuz sits at the centre of the world’s hydrocarbon trade. 
In 2024, around 20 million barrels per day of oil moved through the 
strait, equivalent to roughly 20 percent of global petroleum liquids 
consumption, making it one of the world’s most critical oil transit 
chokepoints. A disruption here interrupts vessel movement, raises 
freight, delays cargo scheduling, and weakens confidence in forward 
supply. Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) traffic through the corridor has 
been severely disrupted due to the current crisis in the Middle East, with 
cargo movements stalling and importers scrambling for replacements. 
For a country like India, where a large share of a welfare-sensitive fuel 
depends on seaborne imports, the global shock has quickly become a 
domestic allocation problem. 
This paper aims to analyze the effects of LPG shortages on the financial 
market, focusing on the advantages of financial markets, the benefits of 
LPG in daily life, and the challenges faced by financial markets during 
an LPG crisis. For this purpose we use some references from 
government sites, tracking oil companies share volatility, evaluating 
Government Interventions, analyzing academic journal as a secondary 
resource and Industries Reports.</jats:p>
