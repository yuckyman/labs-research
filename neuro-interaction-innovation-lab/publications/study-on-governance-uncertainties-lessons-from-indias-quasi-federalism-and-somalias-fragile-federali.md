# Study on Governance Uncertainties: Lessons from India’s Quasi-Federalism and Somalia’s Fragile Federalism

## Abstract

<title>Abstract</title>
                <p>The case study compares the uncertainties in governance between India and Somalia, in terms of their federal governments, and their response to the global developments. We contrast the quasi-federal system of India, which is characterized by centralized coordination and combined with fiscal decentralization against weak federalism of Somalia which cannot cope with the clan-based system of governance and poorly-developed institutional structures. The work is grounded in the comparative design, in accordance with which the efficiency of the government is determined in terms of the following criteria: fiscal federalism, electoral integrity, and the tackling of crises. The Indian system is healthy because it has an orderly revenue sharing and technology based electoral transparency but, executive power is so much dominant that at times it can influence the judicial independence. Comparatively, Somalia is more susceptible to systematic failures like the ad hoc fiscal systems, and poor electoral systems, which have impeded the democratic stability. The findings show that the performance difference on governance is very large with current scenario giving a score of 62.34 with India against 19.07 in Somalia on the voice and accountability index. The adoption of model of fiscal federalism practiced by India and mechanism of responding to the crisis in accordance with the local security issue are some of the lessons that Somalia can learn. The paper presents realistic suggestions to institutional empowerment of weak states that are expected to re-emphasize the relevance of contextual reforms in the fiscal decentralization and the electoral system. A blend of theoretical and practical governance discourse, this research will give a nuanced understanding of why the federal systems can mitigate uncertainties of divergent political settings.</p>
