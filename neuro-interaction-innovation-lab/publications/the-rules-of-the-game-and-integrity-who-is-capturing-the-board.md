# THE RULES OF THE GAME AND INTEGRITY: WHO IS CAPTURING THE BOARD?

## Abstract

<jats:p>The article offers a critical analysis of the interfaces between institutional theory, regulatory capture, and integrity programs, using the metaphor of the “rules of the game” and the regulatory “board” as its central axis. It starts from an institutionalist conception according to which formal and informal institutions shape incentives, structure power relations, and condition development trajectories. In this context, regulatory capture is understood as a possible—and often likely—outcome of institutional arrangements that enable the private appropriation of state regulatory structures. The article then problematizes the role of integrity programs in both the public and private sectors: while they are presented as an institutional response to corruption and capture, they can themselves be co-opted and transformed into symbolic instruments that legitimize an already captured board. Ultimately, the paper argues that any meaningful discussion of integrity in economic law must be grounded in a dense analysis of the institutions that define the rules of the game and of those who, in the last instance, write, apply, and distort them.</jats:p>
