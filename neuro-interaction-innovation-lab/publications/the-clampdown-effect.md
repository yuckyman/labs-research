# The Clampdown Effect

## Abstract

<jats:p>A physical claim rests on three separable supports: internal formal control, operational experimental contact, and reproducible computation. At laboratory scales these three move together. At extreme scales (Planck-regime gravity, cosmic horizons, high-dimensional simulations) they decouple. nWe develop the Clampdown Effect as a precise mathematical theory of that decoupling. A validation score $V_Q\in[0,1]$, formal pressure $W_Q$, operational pressure $S_Q$, and a six-channel error budget $E_Q$ are defined. A Pareto obstruction theorem is proved: under generic inter-channel coupling no admissible theory-instrument-computation triple simultaneously minimises all six error channels. The empirical equivalence classes of $T_Q$ under a fixed corridor threshold carry a natural metric $d_\theta$, and $S_Q$ descends to a Lipschitz-$1$ function on the quotient. Seven foundational axioms are shown to be mutually independent by explicit countermodels. The dynamic corridor criterion, the controlled avoidance theorem, the channel separation bound, and the stochastic Chebyshev corridor complete the calculus.</jats:p>
