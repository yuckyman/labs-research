# Discrete Diffusion for Single-Cell Gene Expression Modeling

## Abstract

<jats:p>
                  Current generative modeling of single-cell transcriptomics relies on continuous latent representations, transforming inherently discrete and sparse gene counts into continuous space. We propose Discrete Cell Models (DCM), a diffusion-based framework that learns cellular representations directly in the discrete domain. Our framework supports both unconditional and conditional generation, allowing for precise modeling of complex biological scenarios such as cell-type-specific transcriptional responses to genetic perturbations. We demonstrate that DCM scales effectively and achieves strong performance against current state-of-the-art methods, including scVI, CPA, STATE, scGPT, and scLDM. On the Dentate Gyrus benchmark, DCM achieves a 5-fold improvement in
                  <jats:italic>MMD^2 RBF</jats:italic>
                  and a nearly 2-fold improvement in
                  <jats:italic>W_2</jats:italic>
                  distance, over the leading continuous diffusion baseline (scLDM). On the conditional Replogle perturbation benchmark, DCM sets a new state of the art on
                  <jats:italic>W_2</jats:italic>
                  distance while remaining competitive on
                  <jats:italic>MMD^2 RBF</jats:italic>
                  . Together, these results establish discrete diffusion as a promising direction for foundational models of cellular biology.
                </jats:p>
