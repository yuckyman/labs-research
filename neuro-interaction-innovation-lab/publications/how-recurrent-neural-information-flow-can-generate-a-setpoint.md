# How Recurrent Neural Information Flow Can Generate a Setpoint

## Abstract

<title>Abstract</title>
                <p>What sets the level to which neural activity returns remains open. In an exact mean-field model of excitatory and inhibitory quadratic integrate-and-fire neurons with fixed connections and no plasticity, target-rate variable or added sensing and effector circuit, we prove that the balance of activity around the excitatory--inhibitory loop fixes a unique level, which is exponentially stable if and only if inhibition feeds the state back to excitatory cells with a gain inside an exact window ($7/16$ to $1187/512$ in a solvable network) and, at unit gain, a delay below a critical value. Applying the data-rate theorem to the nonlinear network, we prove that a feedback channel of capacity $C$ makes the level stable only if $C\ge h$, where $h$ is the rate at which the network's instability generates information about its state (28.9~bits/s in a reference network), and that state-independent signals must fail. In finite simulations of the reference network and random inhibition-stabilized networks, an efficient coder held the level just above $h$, never below. Through a white Gaussian channel, with no other noise, the linearized network's received signal reveals the state at exactly $h$ for every noise amplitude and nonzero gain; by Bode's integral, the same rate fixes the regulation budget against fluctuating input. Spiking networks (10,000 neurons) lost the level under state-independent and rate-limited feedback and gave a cross-spectral integral consistent with $h$. The level resists transient perturbations but shifts under sustained input: it is a settling point, generated by the flow of information through the network.</p>
