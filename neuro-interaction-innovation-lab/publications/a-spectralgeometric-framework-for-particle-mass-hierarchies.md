# A spectral–geometric framework for particle mass hierarchies

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>We develop a spectral--geometric framework in which discrete particle-mass scales are organized by the intrinsic harmonic content of space, rather than being introduced solely through phenomenological parameters. Taking the kinematical arena to be a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold $(\Sigma,\gamma_{ij})$, we argue that locality, diffeomorphism covariance, and second-order (elliptic) spatial dynamics naturally lead to Laplace-type operators and, in the spinorial and vector sectors, to the Dirac and Hodge--de~Rham operators as the geometric objects governing free-field spectra.&amp;#xD;		&amp;#xD;Specializing to the maximally symmetric case $\Sigma\simeq S^{3}(a)$, we derive the scalar, spinor, and vector spectra associated with these operators. Their eigenvalues form rigid discrete towers whose overall scale is controlled by the geometric radius $a$, and which enter as kinematical mass scales in mode decompositions on $\mathbb{R}\times S^{3}$. Interactions enter through harmonic overlap integrals and act as selection and mixing mechanisms among geometric modes.&amp;#xD;		&amp;#xD;The construction yields constrained patterns of hierarchies, degeneracies, and selection rules dictated by harmonic analysis and the representation theory of $\mathrm{SO}(4)\simeq \mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_R$. In this sense, the manuscript provides a precise spectral--geometric template for organizing discrete mass scales and for formulating possible correspondences with particle-physics sectors.&amp;#xD;		&amp;#xD;We emphasize, however, that the present work does not constitute a complete dynamical derivation of Standard-Model masses. Rather, it establishes a semiclassical and kinematical framework in which discrete mass levels emerge from spatial geometry, while a full physical identification requires additional ingredients, including a complete interaction sector, internal gauge structure, and a covariant treatment of the underlying background geometry.</jats:p>
