# El Sufismo en las Noches de Salé

## Abstract

<jats:p>This article investigates the role of Sufism and Ibn Tufail’s figure of Hayy ibn Yaqzan in Las noches de Salé [The Nights of Salé] by Jorge Ruiz Dueñas. It proposes that Sufism operates as a secret method of literary knowledge. Utilizing intertextual and comparative analysis, the study examines Ruiz Dueñas’s narrative strategies: the materialization of myth, spatial relocation, and dream-based communication. We find that the novel transforms Hayy—"the Living One"—from a philosophical allegory into a critical witness acting within the historical and urban setting of Salé. The results demonstrate that Ruiz Dueñas subverts Ibn Tufail’s ascetic path: immortality no longer resides in ontological contemplation, but in an earthly transcendence linked to collective memory and inscription within the city and its rituals. We conclude that literature functions as a ritual of resurrection that denounces the commodification of knowledge and restores an Arab intellectual genealogy often silenced in Western thought.</jats:p>
