# WOUNDS WRITTEN IN WORDS: PSYCHOLOGICAL AND POSTCOLONIAL TRAUMA IN WOMEN-CENTRIC INDIAN ENGLISH FICTION

## Abstract

<jats:p>This paper is about English fiction that talks about women and how it shows trauma in two ways. It looks at the experiences of female characters and the big scars that affect the whole of India because of what happened in the past. India was ruled by another country. Then it was divided, which caused a lot of pain. The paper looks at six novels: Arundhati Roys The God of Small Things Manju Kapurs Daughters Rohinton Mistrys A Fine Balance, Jhumpa Lahiris The Namesake, Salman Rushdies Midnights Children and Meera Syals Anita and Me.It uses ideas from experts on trauma such as Cathy Caruth, Judith Herman and Shoshana Felman and thinkers on issues like Homi Bhabha, Gayatri Spivak and Frantz Fanon. The paper wants to see how Indian English fiction shows that womens bodies, voices and memories are where the personal pain of women and the pain of the nation come together. Indian English fiction is very important here because it helps us understand how women, in India feel about their past and their country.By reading these novels the paper finds six common signs of trauma. When people disconnect from their feelings, physical symptoms, fragmented memories, broken identities passing trauma to the next generation and being silenced. It also says that the trauma caused by Indias history. Partition, violence between communities, the Emergency caste oppression and people being displaced. Does not just happen alongside the experiences of women but actually makes them worse creating wounds that are hard to heal.Indian English fiction that focuses on women is important because it does not just show suffering but also refuses to be silent, about the things that colonial history and patriarchal societies have tried to hide. It brings out the psychological and physical truths of women-centric Indian English fiction and the people who have been ignored by the main stories of the nation. Women-centric Indian English fiction is vital because it recovers the truths of women- Indian English fiction and the people who have been overlooked for a long time.</jats:p>
