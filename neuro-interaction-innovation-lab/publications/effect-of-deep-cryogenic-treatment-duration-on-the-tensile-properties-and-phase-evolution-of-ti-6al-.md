# Effect of Deep Cryogenic Treatment Duration on the Tensile Properties and Phase Evolution of Ti-6Al-4V ELI Alloy

## Abstract

<jats:p>The present study evaluated the effect of soaking time during deep cryogenic treatment (DCT) on the tensile behavior and phase evolution of the Ti-6Al-4V ELI alloy. Samples were subjected to different cryogenic exposure durations, and their mechanical response was assessed by tensile testing, while changes in the microstructure were investigated by optical microscopy. Compared with the as-received condition, the yield stress exhibited a pronounced time-dependent response, increasing after 1 h of soaking and subsequently decreasing with longer treatment durations, reaching its lowest value at 12 h. The elastic modulus also showed a non-monotonic variation with soaking time, suggesting that cryogenic exposure modifies the mechanical response through microstructural changes rather than by a simple time-dependent strengthening effect. Similarly, the ultimate tensile strength and rupture stress varied with treatment duration, indicating that DCT can modify the balance between strength and ductility of the alloy. Optical microscopy revealed time-dependent changes in the α/β phase morphology. Specifically, a reduction in the β-phase fraction was observed after 1 h of treatment, whereas longer exposure promoted microstructural rearrangement and coarser reprecipitation, particularly after 12 h. The combined mechanical and microstructural results indicate that the response of Ti-6Al-4V ELI to deep cryogenic treatment is strongly dependent on soaking time. These findings demonstrate that treatment duration is a critical processing parameter and should be carefully optimized when DCT is employed to tailor the mechanical performance and microstructural characteristics of Ti-6Al-4V ELI alloy for engineering and biomedical applications, particularly where predictable mechanical behavior and long-term material reliability are required.</jats:p>
