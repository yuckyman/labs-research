# Machine learning-based regression and time-series forecasting of reflectivity trends in regenerated fibre Bragg gratings at different thermal regeneration conditions

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>
                    The paper proposes an integrated machine learning framework that combines regression-based modeling and deep learning–based time-series forecasting to analyze and predict the reflectivity evolution of regenerated fibre Bragg gratings across multiple regeneration temperatures of 800 °C, 850 °C, and 900 °C. Chronological and random splitting with cross-validation were employed to ensure robust model training, evaluation, and generalization. Multiple regression models, including baseline approaches, were implemented and systematically compared. Nonlinear temporal variation in reflectivity, particularly under different temperatures, reduces the effectiveness of simple models (LR, PLR). In contrast, tree-based and instance-based models (KNN, GBR, DT, RF) capture complex relationships more effectively, yielding improved accuracy and R
                    <jats:sup>2</jats:sup>
                    values. In addition, time-series forecasting methods such as LSTM, GRU, CNN, and their hybrid architectures capture temporal dependencies and evolving patterns in the reflectivity sequence. These approaches support long-range forecasting beyond the experimental window, enabling the projection of reflectivity behaviour up to extended operating durations (e.g., 1000 h). The robust performance of LSTM and GRU-based models highlights their suitability for real-time predictive analysis and experimental monitoring. Collectively, regression and time-series techniques offer a comprehensive framework for reflectivity analysis, with regression models providing accurate functional representation within the dataset and time-series models enabling reliable long-term trend prediction. Model performance was evaluated using optimal hyperparameter configurations balancing accuracy and generalization. Moreover, anomaly detection performed across three temperature parameters indicates that temperature plays a critical role in determining the nature and frequency of abnormal reflectivity behaviour. A comparative evaluation of anomaly detection models, including Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM, Autoencoders, and LSTM-encoders, shows that LSTM encoders outperform other models due to their ability to model temporal dependencies. Finally, this approach enables accurate interpolation within experimental ranges, reliable long-duration forecasting, and robust anomaly detection, supporting predictive monitoring of fibre optic sensing systems. The approach also serves as an effective complement to traditional approaches, particularly in applications demanding rapid prediction or constrained by limited physical insight.
