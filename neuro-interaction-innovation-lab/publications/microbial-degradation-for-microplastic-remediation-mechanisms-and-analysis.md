# Microbial Degradation for Microplastic Remediation: Mechanisms and Analysis

## Abstract

<jats:p>Microbial degradation presents an eco-friendly and sustainable approach to addressing microplastic (MP) pollution. This section explores the role of microorganisms, including bacteria, fungi, and archaea, in breaking down synthetic polymers through enzymatic actions such as oxygenases, esterases, and hydrolases. Key species like Pseudomonas, Ideonella sakaiensis, Aspergillus, and Rhodococcus are emphasized. The impact of environmental factors, including temperature, salinity, and nutrient levels, on degradation efficiency is discussed. Additionally, advanced analytical tools like FTIR, Raman spectroscopy, and SEM aid in assessing polymer breakdown. Overcoming challenges like slow degradation rates requires innovative bioengineering strategies for effective MP bioremediation.</jats:p>
