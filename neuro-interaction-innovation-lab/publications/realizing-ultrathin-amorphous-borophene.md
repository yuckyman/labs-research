# Realizing Ultrathin Amorphous Borophene:

## Abstract

<jats:p>Amorphous nanomaterials are an interesting subset of materials with properties that are derived from the lack of long-range atomic order found in crystalline structures. The absence of an ordered lattice provides distinct advantages, such as high isotropic conductivity, superior mechanical toughness, and a high density of unsaturated surface sites that open pathways for surface modification. Borophene (BP) is a synthetic, two-dimensional allotrope of boron that has captured intense scientific interest because it is stronger, lighter, and more flexible than graphene. While crystalline forms of BP have been widely explored, the amorphous phase of BP has remained experimentally elusive. Here, we report the first synthesis of ultrathin amorphous BP (aBP) via liquid-phase exfoliation of amorphous boron nanoparticles and the covalent 2 modification of the surface of the resulting nanomaterial. The aBP nanodisks are less than 2 nm thick and do not exhibit long-range order. The reaction of the aBP nanodisks with aryl diazonium salts results in the spontaneous attachment of aryl groups to the nanomaterial surface, as confirmed by several techniques. We propose a radical-mediated mechanism in which surfacegenerated aryl radicals react with electronically active, defect-rich sites on aBP. This mild and versatile strategy establishes a new platform for engineering aBP properties and expands its potential in electronic, sensing, and catalytic applications.</jats:p>
