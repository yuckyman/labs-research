# Agroecologia, imaginário e complexidade

## Abstract

<jats:p>This text stems from the mythological, philosophical, and historical roots of Western civilization that underpin the symbolic rupture between humanity and the natural world, reinforced by the episteme of modern science, whose resulting social imaginary justifies the predatory exploitation of nature. The legacy of Cartesian rationalism has consolidated a worldview based on fragmentation, simplification, and linearity, which hinders the perception of the complex systems inherent in life. This theoretical essay aims to present principles of complex thought and systemic vision, and to use the agricultural management of the Mebêngôkre people as a practical example of dialogue between science and traditional knowledge to establish an ecology of knowledge that promotes a new understanding of reality, aligned with agroecological principles. The work advocates an epistemological transition, in which agroecology acts as a transversal field capable of overcoming the imaginary of domination in favor of an ethical and ecological coexistence with the biosphere.</jats:p>
