# Embodied knowing at the borderlands:
                    <i>Autohistoria-teoría</i>
                    and the lived story of a Brazilian Olympic athlete

## Abstract

<jats:p>
                    In this article, I explore the potential of Gloria Anzaldúa's
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">autohistoria-teoría</jats:italic>
                    as a decolonial methodology for qualitative research in sport. Drawing on an in-depth narrative with Brazilian Olympic rugby athlete Taís Bernal Balconi, I examine how athletic experience can illuminate Anzaldúa's stages of
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">conocimiento</jats:italic>
                    :
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">arrebato</jats:italic>
                    (rupture),
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">nepantla</jats:italic>
                    (contradiction), and
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">Coyolxauhqui</jats:italic>
                    (recomposition). I interpret the athlete's reflections on injury, exclusion, and healing as forms of embodied theorizing—moments in which the body itself becomes a site of ethical, emotional, and spiritual knowledge. By reading sport through
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">autohistoria-teoría</jats:italic>
                    , I reframe the athlete's narrative not as data but as theory-in-motion. I show how listening, vulnerability, and relational care emerge as epistemic practices that challenge performance-driven paradigms in sport research. The study contributes to ongoing feminist and decolonial debates by proposing
                    <jats:italic toggle="yes">autohistoria-teoría</jats:italic>
                    as a relational and transformative methodology that recognizes the body as both subject and source of knowledge.
                  </jats:p>
