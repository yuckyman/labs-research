# BETWEEN LOVE AND CAPITAL: THE FAMILY AS A STRATEGY FOR CONTROL AND THE PRODUCTION OF SUBJECTIVITIES

## Abstract

<jats:p>ABSTRACTAdopting a qualitative approach, this essay aimed to present the theoretical constructs related to the tensions and disputes surrounding attempts to define the family, seeking to demonstrate the possible conceptual foundations responsible for its politicization in the contemporary context. Based on a review of the literature, it was possible to reflect on the family both from the analytical perspective of social theory authors and through contemporary conceptions that highlight the plurality of family formations and the transformations that have occurred in social relations. In this process, neoliberalism, neoconservatism, and familism emerged as central foundations for understanding these disputes—rationalities that directly influence social organization and current political discourses. The results indicate that neoliberal and neoconservative rationalities play a significant role not only in maintaining market logic and conservative moral values but also in the construction of individual and collective subjectivities. Neoliberalism reinforces individual accountability and transfers to the family functions previously attributed to the state, while neoconservatism strengthens traditional and normative family models. Thus, it is concluded that the family transcends the status of a private institution, becoming a space for ideological, political, and social disputes in contemporary society.</jats:p>
