# Teacher Competence and the Effectiveness of the Multi-Factored Assessment Tool in Advancing Inclusive Education in Philippine Public Schools

## Abstract

<jats:p>This study looked into how the 77 respondents of the study apply inclusive education practices, the extent to which the Multi-Factored Assessment Tool (MFAT) is used, and how effective it is in supporting learner diversity in public elementary schools in Bunawan District, Agusan del Sur, Philippines. Drawing on Instructional, Transformational, and Distributed Leadership theories and guided by Department of Education (DepEd) mandates, the research used a quantitative design with a validated researcher-developed questionnaire. Data were analyzed using frequency counts, mean scores, Pearson correlation, and one-way ANOVA, with descriptive statistics and difference testing at the 0.05 level of significance. The findings revealed clear gaps between professional development and classroom practice. Despite frequent training, many teachers felt unprepared to fully implement inclusive approaches. Pearson correlation showed strong links among teacher competence, inclusive practices, and MFAT effectiveness, highlighting competence as the crucial bridge between policy and practice. One-way ANOVA further indicated differences across teacher groups, pointing to the need for tailored support.This study is among the first to quantitatively assess MFAT effectiveness in Philippine classrooms, adding to global conversations on data-driven inclusive education. Results underscore the importance of leadership support, mentoring, and continuous professional development. Policy implications suggest that education systems, especially in resource-constrained settings, must prioritize differentiated faculty development, manageable workloads, and systemic alignment to transform MFAT from a compliance tool into a meaningful instrument for equity and inclusion.</jats:p>
