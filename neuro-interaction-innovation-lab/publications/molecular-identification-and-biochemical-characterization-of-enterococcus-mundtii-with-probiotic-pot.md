# Molecular identification and biochemical characterization of Enterococcus mundtii with probiotic potential and microbial activity isolated from the intestinal microbiome of Comadia redtenbacheri (Lepidoptera: Cossidae)

## Abstract

<jats:p>Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) improve the diet of animals and humans, but their probiotic capacity depends on the source of isolation and species of the microorganism. Therefore, exploring new sources of probiotics is necessary due to their food and economic importance. This study aimed to characterize and identify a LAB isolated from the intestinal microbiome of Comadia redtenbacheri (larva that infects Agave salmiana). It was determined, catalase, amylase, protease, lipase, gelatinase and hemolytic activity, glucose, lactose, maltose, and sucrose fermentation capacity, effect of pH and temperature on growth, lactic acid and exopolysaccharide production, phenol resistance (0.2 and 0.5%), pancreatin (0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.5, 3.0 and 6.0%), bile salts (2%), antagonistic activity (Escherichia coli, Salmonella enteritidis and Salmonella typhimurium) and identification by molecular methods. The strain showed growth at 28 and 37°C at pH 1 to 8, with production of lactic acid and exopolysaccharides, resistance to phenol, bile salts and pancreatin. Amylase, protease, lipase, gelatinase and negative hemolytic activity were also observed. Culture supernatants at pH 4.6 showed high inhibition against E. coli, S. enteritidis and S. typhimurium (92.7% to 93%). Molecular analysis identified the strain as Enterococcus mundtii. This is the first report of the isolation of E. mundtii from C. redtenbacheri, and the results suggest its possible use as a probiotic for the formulation of a biopreparation within the food industry.</jats:p>
