# Democracy in Retreat: Structural and Historical Drivers of Eastern European Politics

## Abstract

<jats:p>This article examines democratic backsliding in Eastern Europe through a mechanism-based framework that integrates internal institutional weaknesses, ethnopopulist legitimation, and external hybrid pressures, with particular attention to the law–politics interface. Focusing on Hungary, Poland, and Moldova, the study conceptualises regression as “backsliding by law,” whereby incumbents preserve electoral procedures while incrementally converting the rule of law into an instrument of executive entrenchment through judicial governance reforms, oversight capture, and administrative reconfiguration. The analysis synthesises research showing that post-communist institutional opportunity structures—weak checks and balances, corruption, and clientelist governance—lower the costs of institutional conversion and facilitate the consolidation of aspiring dominant-party regimes. It further demonstrates how ethnopopulism supplies a legitimating infrastructure for illiberal reforms by reframing liberal constraints as obstacles to popular sovereignty and by intensifying regime divides and affective polarisation. External pressures are treated as conditional amplification rather than a standalone cause. While Russia-linked disinformation and political penetration are widely discussed, the evidence for direct authoritarian diffusion remains contested; external influence appears most consequential where domestic institutions are already weak and where incumbents can operationalise sovereignty narratives. The article also highlights the ambivalent role of the European Union: integration may constrain illiberalism yet unintentionally enable executive strengthening and weak domestic institutions, thereby facilitating strategic compliance and continued institutional conversion. The study contributes by specifying interaction effects among capture, legitimation, and amplification mechanisms and by identifying research gaps concerning Moldova, local-level dynamics, and the long-term institutional consequences of EU interventions.</jats:p>
