# Instructional Leadership Competence and Practices Among School Administrators and Teachers

## Abstract

<jats:p>Instructional leadership is pivotal in advancing teaching quality and learner achievement, yet rural schools continue to grapple with challenges such as limited mentoring opportunities, inconsistent supervision, and heavy administrative workload. This study investigated the competence and practices of school administrators and teachers in Bunawan District, Division of Agusan del Sur, employing a validated survey instrument administered to 60 respondents. Statistical analyses included frequency distribution, weighted mean, Pearson correlation, and ANOVA. Findings revealed high levels of instructional leadership competence (composite mean = 4.02) and practice implementation (composite mean = 4.01). Significant correlations were observed between respondents’ demographic profiles and competence (p &lt; 0.05), as well as between competence and practice implementation (p &lt; 0.05). Moreover, perceptual differences in practice implementation across groups were statistically significant. These results underscore the imperative to strengthen instructional supervision, mentoring systems, and sustained professional development programs to ensure effective teaching and improved learner outcomes in rural school contexts. The study contributes to the discourse on rural educational leadership by highlighting the interplay between competence, practice, and contextual challenges, offering insights for policy and capacity-building initiatives.</jats:p>
