# Cartographers of the unknown: Transitional justice as a map for imagining Syria after Assad

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract∞</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>This article examines Syrian civil society’s use of transitional justice as an anticipatory project to envision a new polity, and the role of justice in achieving this aspiration. Based on document analysis and interviews, the article identifies different understandings of breaking with the past and healing, which inform diverging paths for the future. By exploring these conceptual divergences, the article argues that transitional justice is a semiotically unstable tool for imagining the future, as it accommodates different justice imaginaries with potentially conflicting agendas. The article analyzes civil society’s efforts as cartographies for the future, and charts the divergences along justice imaginaries familiar to the transitional justice field: retributivist, institutionalist and communitarian. By discussing compatibilities and tensions between imaginaries, this article offers a deep-seated analysis of Syrian civil society’s embrace of transitional justice and demonstrates transitional justice’s potential as an interpretative framework.</jats:p>
