# An evaluation of in-service abrasion of subsea power cables situated in shallow water

## Abstract

<title>Abstract</title>
                <p>Subsea cable abrasion occurs when the protective outer layers of a cable are worn away due to contact with the seabed or other objects, often caused by hydrodynamic forces like waves and currents. This study experimentally assesses the in-service abrasion of a subsea cable and validates the results with numerical estimations for a cable located in the Lower Zakum oil field. The abrasion resistance test for cables has been established and executed with specified acceptance criteria. The outside layer composed of polypropylene (PP) yarn has been infused with bitumen. The numerical results indicate that a feasible cable route has been evaluated for cumulative displacement and the incidence of abrasive wear cycles. The identical cable has been evaluated for the planned armoring and its efficacy. Metallic and non-metallic armor has been employed and evaluated to safeguard cables from operational abrasion.</p>
