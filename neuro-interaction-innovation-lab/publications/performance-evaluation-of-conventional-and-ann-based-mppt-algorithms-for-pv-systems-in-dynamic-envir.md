# Performance evaluation of conventional and ANN-based MPPT algorithms for PV systems in dynamic environmental conditions

## Abstract

<jats:p>
                    This paper presents a rigorous performance comparison of five maximum power point tracking (MPPT) algorithms for photovoltaic (PV) systems under environmental conditions using MATLAB/Simulink. The methods evaluated include perturb and observe (P&amp;O), incremental conductance (INC), fractional open circuit voltage (FOCV), fractional short circuit current (FSCC), a modern optimization-based grey wolf optimizer (GWO), and an artificial neural network (ANN)-based controller. Conventional MPPT methods often show reduced performance, experiencing delayed tracking, increased oscillations, and significant energy loss, particularly during rapid variations in irradiance and temperature. To address these limitations, a predictive ANN-based MPPT controller is developed using a dynamically generated dataset derived from varying irradiance and temperature profiles. Unlike conventional iterative methods, the proposed ANN directly estimates the optimal operating voltage (V
                    <jats:sub>MPP</jats:sub>
                    ), enabling faster convergence and improved stability. Simulation results demonstrate that the ANN-based controller achieves the highest efficiency (97.55%), the fastest transient response (20–30 ms), and minimal steady-state power fluctuation (&lt;5 W) among all evaluated MPPT techniques. The GWO-based method also shows improved performance over conventional techniques but remains slightly inferior to the ANN-based approach. These results confirm that the proposed ANN-based MPPT controller provides a robust and efficient solution for PV systems operating under rapidly changing environmental conditions.
