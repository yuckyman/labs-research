# Timor-Leste Government Policies Toward Government Land Used By Foreign Companies

## Abstract

<jats:p>Background: This study examines government policies related to state land management, with a focus on land use by foreign companies within the legal framework of Timor-Leste. State land is a critical issue related to state sovereignty and the regulation of natural resource utilization.
Objective: This study aims to explore the land asset regulation system under the Constitution of the Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste (RDTL), as well as the government's role in managing state land. This study also aims to analyze the legal basis for the nationalization of state assets and the challenges faced in implementing this policy.
Method: This research approach uses normative legal methods, examining laws and regulations governing land ownership and use in Timor-Leste, as well as analyzing government policies on state land management.
Results: This study finds that the Timor-Leste government, through the National Directorate of Land and Property of the Ministry of Justice, has made efforts to regulate land ownership, including a policy of nationalizing state assets involving land owned by foreign companies. However, significant challenges remain in implementing this policy, particularly related to suboptimal law enforcement.
Conclusion: The implementation of land policy in Timor-Leste requires a more systematic approach to managing and administering state assets. More intensive efforts are needed to enforce existing regulations so that this policy can be implemented effectively, especially in land management involving foreign companies under the framework of state sovereignty.</jats:p>
