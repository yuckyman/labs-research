# N, S-Codoped
Carbon Quantum Dots Derived from
EDTA and Sulfanilamide for the Detection of Chromium(VI)

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>The detection of toxic heavy metals, e.g., Cr(VI), is crucial for the environment and human health. Two carbon dots (CDs) derived from disodium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) and Sulfanilamide (SNM) at two different ratios have been synthesized and investigated for Cr(VI) metal-sensing applications. The alteration of the sulfanilamide concentration for CD preparation implies the effect of functionalization on Cr(VI) sensing. The ratio of EDTA and SNM was considered 1:1 and 2:1, and the carbon dots synthesized were designated as CD11 and CD21. CD11 was able to detect Cr(VI) through fluorescence quenching even at a low Cr(VI) concentration of 0.32 μM (limit of detection). The Stern–Volmer plot, average lifetime measurements, and UV–visible experiments revealed that the fluorescence quenching originated from a pure static quenching mechanism, as the bimolecular quenching constant values were obtained close to 1013, with an alteration in the UV peak maxima and negligible change in the average lifetime. The quenching mechanism as a pure static mechanism was also confirmed by IFE correction of fluorescence intensities and quenching efficiencies of both CDs. The real sample analysis for Cr(VI) sensing verified the sensitivity of the fluorescence method and the practical usability of the CDs. Herein, we demonstrate a technique to recognize Cr(VI) in the solution medium with N, S-codoped fluorescent carbon dots.</jats:p>
