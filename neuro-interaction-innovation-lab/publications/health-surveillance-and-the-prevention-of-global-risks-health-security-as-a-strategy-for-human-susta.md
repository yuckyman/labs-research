# Health surveillance and the prevention of global risks: health security as a strategy for human sustainability

## Abstract

<jats:p>The intensification of global flows of people, goods and pathogens has redefined public health as a problem of interdependence: a localized health risk may, within a few weeks, become an emergency of international concern. This article discusses the role of health surveillance in preventing global risks and argues that health security constitutes a strategy for human sustainability. This is a narrative review of the literature, with a qualitative approach, based on scientific sources and national and international normative documents published between 1988 and 2026. The results were organized into five analytical axes: surveillance as infrastructure for collective security; the logic of anticipation in the face of emerging global risks; scientific-institutional mechanisms and network governance; the One Health approach as a basis for sustainability; and equity as a condition of effectiveness. It is concluded that surveillance should not be understood as an administrative cost, but as strategic infrastructure whose function is to convert data into timely decisions, reduce population vulnerabilities and sustain collective life in an interdependent world. The need to strengthen early detection, evidence-informed policies and intersectoral articulation is reinforced, aligning health security with the commitments of the 2030 Agenda.</jats:p>
