# Encapsulation of small molecules by Porous Fullerenes

## Abstract

<title>Abstract</title>
                <p>Context\\In the present work we investigate one class of closed-cage molecules proposed a few years ago, known as Porous Fullerenes. Our study aims to clarify if those molecules could be used to encapsulate small molecules and, once formed, if such complex could be stable. We found that one of the porous fullerenes, namely BPCC\((_{20})\), could hold up to 16CO\((_2)\), 24H\((_2)\)O or 36H\((_2)\) at room temperature, while PGC\((_{20})\) could hold up to 24CO\((_2)\), 48H\((_2)\)O or 58H\((_2)\) molecules.The inner region of the complex resulted in an environment in which the captured molecules are subjected to pressure exerted by the cage, resulting in spatial arrangements more compact than those found for the same molecular clusters in vacuum. These compacted geometries could allow the realization of chemical reactions that may be difficult to obtain in a free environment. Methods\\The atomic structure and electronic states were studied using the Self-Consistent Charge Density Functional Tight Binding (SCC-DFTB) version of the DFTB approximation as implemented within the framework of the DFTB+ open-source software. Molecular dynamics simulations were performed in a NVT ensemble, with temperatures being controlled by a Nose-Hoover Thermostat. The adopted DFTB parametrization was validated by comparing small scale calculations made within DFTB against DFT calculations carried using the software CP2K. For DFT, the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof(PBE) exchange-correlation potential was used considering the Goedecker–Teter–Hutter (GTH-PBE) pseudopotentials and the double-zeta valence (DZVP-MOLOPT-SR-GTH) basis set.</p>
