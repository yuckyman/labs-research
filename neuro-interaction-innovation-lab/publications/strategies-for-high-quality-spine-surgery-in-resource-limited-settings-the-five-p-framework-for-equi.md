# Strategies for High-Quality Spine Surgery in Resource-Limited Settings: The Five-P Framework for Equitable Surgical Delivery

## Abstract

<jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Study Design</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Narrative review with a Conceptual Framework.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Objectives</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), home to approximately 80–85% of the global population, face a disproportionate burden of spinal disorders and limited access to timely, safe, and affordable surgical care. This review proposes a practical framework for delivering high-value spine care in LMICs by integrating evidence on health systems, value-based healthcare, and spine surgery into five strategic domains: Policy &amp; Governance, Patient Selection, Perioperative Efficiency, Products &amp; Technology, and Protection (the Five-P Framework).</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Methods</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>A narrative review with a structured literature search was conducted using PubMed and gray literature. Studies on spinal care delivery, health systems, cost-effectiveness, and surgical strategies in LMICs or comparable settings were qualitatively synthesized into thematic domains.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Results</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>
                      Five domains were identified: (1)
                      <jats:bold>P</jats:bold>
                      olicy &amp; Governance, including centralization of care, regulation of financial incentives, anti-corruption measures, and complication reduction; (2)
                      <jats:bold>P</jats:bold>
                      atient Selection, emphasizing evidence-based indications; (3)
                      <jats:bold>P</jats:bold>
                      erioperative Efficiency, including enhanced recovery pathways, ambulatory surgery, and standardized protocols; (4)
                      <jats:bold>P</jats:bold>
                      roducts &amp; Technology, focusing on resource optimization and context-appropriate technologies, including locally manufactured implants; and (5)
                      <jats:bold>P</jats:bold>
                      rotection, promoting balanced medicolegal frameworks. Together, these domains aim to maximize value, improve access, and reduce unnecessary procedures and inefficient resource utilization.
                    </jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Conclusion</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>The Five-P Framework is a theoretical model that provides a system-level strategy to improve access to high-value spine care in LMICs through appropriate patient selection, efficient perioperative care, and optimal use of limited resources. Future studies to validate this proposal are necessary.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
