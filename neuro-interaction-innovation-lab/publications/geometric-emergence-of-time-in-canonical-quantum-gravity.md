# Geometric Emergence of Time in Canonical Quantum Gravity

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>The absence of a preferred time variable in generally covariant systems lies at the heart of the canonical problem of time in quantum gravity. In this work we develop a fully canonical and quantum-consistent framework that identifies the geometric conditions under which a relational description of time becomes valid, and the conditions under which it fails. Starting from unmodified General Relativity, we introduce a geometric clock field ϕ that does not modify the Einstein–Hilbert action and does not add new dynamical degrees of freedom. The clock is fixed by a geometric condition tying its gradient to the foliation normal, with a clock rate N [γ, K] constructed from&amp;#xD;intrinsic curvature and irreducible extrinsic geometric invariants.&amp;#xD;Within Dirac quantization, the Wheeler–DeWitt equation admits a controlled deparametrization with respect to ϕ in regimes where N &gt; 0, yielding a relational Schr¨odinger equation whose Hamiltonian is entirely determined by spatial geometry. Crucially, the framework identifies not only the domain of validity but also the breakdown of time as a well-defined ordering parameter: when&amp;#xD;N → 0, relational evolution ceases to be globally valid and the description reverts smoothly to a constraint-based formulation.&amp;#xD;Solvable minisuperspace models demonstrate that the geometric clock is rigid in strongly curved regimes, such as the early universe, reproducing standard cosmological results, while temporal ordering weakens in asymptotically flat or weakly curved sectors. These results provide a concrete and conservative resolution of the timelessness problem, showing that time is neither fundamental nor&amp;#xD;universally available, but a regime-dependent relational structure whose availability is controlled by spatial geometry.</jats:p>
