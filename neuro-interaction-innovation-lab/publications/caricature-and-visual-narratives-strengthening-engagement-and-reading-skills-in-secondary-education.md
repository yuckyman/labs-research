# Caricature and Visual Narratives: Strengthening Engagement and Reading Skills in Secondary Education

## Abstract

<jats:p>Reading instruction continues to evolve through creative and learner-centered strategies, prompting this study to investigate the pedagogical value of caricature and visual narratives in secondary education. Using a descriptive-correlational quantitative design, data were collected from 204 teachers through stratified proportional random sampling and from 117 students through complete enumeration. Statistical analyses included frequency, percentage, mean, standard deviation, Pearson product-moment correlation, and analysis of variance (ANOVA) to examine integration levels, perceptions, and their relationship to student engagement. Results revealed high levels of integration of caricatures and visual narratives in reading instruction, accompanied by strong student engagement, particularly in self-reported reading improvement and self-efficacy. Significant correlations emerged between the extent of integration and student engagement, with notable differences across teacher profile variables. These findings affirm the potential of visual learning strategies to enhance reading skills and foster meaningful engagement among secondary learners.</jats:p>
