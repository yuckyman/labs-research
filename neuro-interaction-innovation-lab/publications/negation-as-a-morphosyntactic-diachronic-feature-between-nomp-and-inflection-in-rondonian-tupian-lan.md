# Negation as a morphosyntactic diachronic feature between NomP and inflection in Rondonian Tupian languages

## Abstract

<jats:p>This article shows evidence that some negation morphemes in Tupian languages share two diachronic behaviors when selecting adjectives/adverbial environments in low or high stages of syntactic derivation. We can also observe that these diachronic behaviors have been evolving to defective nominalizations in high syntactic stages, to traditional Negation, or to complete nominalizations in low syntactic stages, also to traditional Negation, except in Tupari, as posed in Singerman (2018b). Singerman argues that, for sentential Negation, it must be affixed a nominalization suffix {-ɾo-} before the Negation {-ʔom}, in low stages of the derivation. We postulate that similar phenomena should have happened in Wayoro, Akuntsu, and Makurap. In Karitiana and Sakurabiat, we only have high syntactic constructions based on the nominalizer *-ap, its traditional place of appearance (Bento 2024), together with a murky morphological content *-nV. Gavião and its adjectival morpheme of “non-existentiality” {ṍòp}, derived from a verbal root {-õò-} (Moore 1984), together with the tendency of the *-ap morpheme to make adverbial constructions of circumstance (Cinque 1999) with *-ap derived pospositions adds to the diachronic investigation a range between circumstantial PPs, defective nominalizations, adjectival Negation to complete nominalizations, and traditional Negation as well. Over time, each of these languages changed to other types of Negation, and only Tupari retained the combined use of sentential Negation with Nominalization. These phenomena can be demonstrated by some tools of internal reconstruction and by some elements of comparative reconstruction (Trask 1996; Campbell 1999).</jats:p>
