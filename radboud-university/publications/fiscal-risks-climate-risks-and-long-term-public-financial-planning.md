# Fiscal Risks, Climate Risks, and Long-Term Public Financial Planning

## Abstract

<jats:p>Agriculture in developing economies is increasingly vulnerable to climate shocks, pest outbreaks, and market volatility, all of which destabilize food security and fiscal stability. While agricultural insurance and risk management frameworks offer resilience, their effectiveness is undermined by the agricultural digital divide, which excludes smallholders from financial platforms and climate-responsive budgeting. This chapter examines the intersection of fiscal risks, climate governance, and digital equity, drawing on resilience theory, new institutional economics, and cultural ecology. Using mixed methods and case studies from Zimbabwe, Kenya, India, and OECD countries, it highlights how digital exclusion weakens insurance uptake and fiscal planning. Policy recommendations emphasize embedding digital inclusion into fiscal governance, investing in rural infrastructure, and expanding public-private partnerships to strengthen resilience.</jats:p>
