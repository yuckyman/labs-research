# Business is personal: How CEO personality influences agency costs

## Abstract

                    <jats:title>Research Summary</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>
                      We examine whether
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      personality traits influence the likelihood of engaging in behaviors that impose agency costs on the firm. We use an established open‐language machine learning program to measure
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      Big Five personality traits based on
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>

                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEOs</jats:styled-content>
                      are associated with lower agency costs (operationalized here as unrelated diversification and the decoupling of
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      pay from firm performance), while higher levels of neuroticism are associated with higher agency costs. By revealing how
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      personality traits affect agency costs, our study offers a new perspective on the sources of agency problems and broadens the application of
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      Big Five personality traits to corporate governance.
                    </jats:p>

                    <jats:p>
                      This study explores how a
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      's personality can shape decisions that affect company performance. Using an advanced language analysis tool, we assessed
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEOs</jats:styled-content>
                      ' personality traits based on what they say during earnings calls. We find that
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEOs</jats:styled-content>

                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEOs</jats:styled-content>
                      with higher levels of neuroticism are more likely to engage in these costly behaviors. These findings suggest that understanding a
                      <jats:styled-content style="fixed-case">CEO</jats:styled-content>
                      's personality can offer valuable insights into their leadership behavior and help boards and investors make better governance and hiring decisions.
