# Smallholder Farmers’ Perception and Adaptation to Climate Change in the Lake Victoria Basin, Kenya: A Mixed-Method Approach

## Abstract

<title>Abstract</title>
                <p>This paper explores smallholder farmers’ perceptions of climate change, identifies main adaptation strategies, and analyses factors influencing adaptation choices in Kenya’s Lake Victoria Basin. The study used a cross-sectional survey of 323 households, with data collected through a semi-structured questionnaire and Focused Group Discussions (FGDs). The Heckman probit model identified factors influencing perception and adaptation; FGDs were analysed using content analysis. The study provides empirical evidence that the majority of the farmers in the study area are aware of climate change. Results from a Heckman probit model indicate that age, gender, income, market distance, livestock ownership, land size, and farming systems influence climate change perception. Adoption of agroforestry, mixed cropping, and improved varieties is shaped by gender, household size, education level, market distance, livestock ownership, group membership, access to credit, and farming systems. Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) constructs: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions influenced behavioural intentions towards adaptation. Social influence and facilitating conditions were the primary drivers of adaptation, whereas the main barriers included unpredictable weather, labour shortages, and cultural factors. The study recommends strengthening training and extension services, particularly for less-educated smallholders, and targeted institutional, policy, and technology support are needed to reduce barriers to land access, investment costs, and climate uncertainty. Moreover, promoting youth engagement in farming can enhance adoption rates and promote sustainable livelihoods.</p>
