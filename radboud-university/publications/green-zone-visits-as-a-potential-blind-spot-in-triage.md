# Green Zone Visits as a Potential Blind Spot in Triage

## Abstract

<jats:p xml:lang="en">Aim:To discuss emergency department green zone visits beyond the perspective of “unnecessary use” and to address their potential role as a blind spot within triage systems.Materials and Methods:A literature-synthesized approach was adopted, focusing on low-acuity emergency department visits, triage systems, and healthcare utilization. Green zone presentations were evaluated from a system-oriented perspective.Results:In the existing literature, green zone visits are predominantly defined as inappropriate or non-urgent presentations. However, available evidence indicates that low-acuity triage classification does not always correspond to low clinical risk, particularly in patients in the early stages of disease, those presenting with atypical symptoms, and socially vulnerable populations. In addition, system-level factors such as access to primary care services and health literacy were found to significantly influence green zone utilization.Conclusion:Green zone visits should not be regarded solely as a burden to be reduced, but rather as a meaningful indicator reflecting both the limitations of triage systems and the overall functioning of the healthcare system. This perspective may contribute to more effective triage practices and improved healthcare service planning.Keywords:Emergency department; Green zone; Triage; Healthcare utilization</jats:p>
