# Determinants of Blue-Collar Workers’ Participation in a Sector-Initiated Worksite Health Promotion Program in Freight Transport: An Observational Study

## Abstract

<jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Purpose</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Worksite health promotion programs (WHPPs) can promote a healthier lifestyle for blue-collar workers, yet their participation is generally low. From a social ecological perspective, this study aims to identify determinants associated with blue-collar workers’ initial WHPP participation.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Design</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Observational study.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Setting</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>A freight transport sector-initiated WHPP in the Netherlands.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Participants</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>The study sample included 3916 blue-collar workers. Data from 976 white-collar workers were used for comparisons.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Measures</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Data were obtained from an online health survey as part of the WHPP. Participation in a follow-up intervention was measured by attendance registration of the first consultation.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Analysis</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Associations between determinants and participation were tested, using logistic regression. Interactions of determinants with job group were studied to explore differences in associations with participation between blue- and white-collar workers.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Results</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>
                      The final multivariable logistic model identified contract security (
                      <jats:italic toggle="yes">OR</jats:italic>
                      = 2.06), a neutral work-life balance (
                      <jats:italic toggle="yes">OR</jats:italic>
                      = 1.24), more chronic conditions (
                      <jats:italic toggle="yes">OR</jats:italic>
                      = 1.67), poorer self-rated health (
                      <jats:italic toggle="yes">OR</jats:italic>
                      = 2.17), and insufficient sleep (
                      <jats:italic toggle="yes">OR</jats:italic>
                      = 1.24) as being associated with WHPP participation. The determinants were largely consistent across blue- and white-collar workers. The model showed good fit, but provided little explanatory power (Nagelkerke R
                      <jats:sup>2</jats:sup>
                      = .058).
                    </jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Conclusion</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>While a sector-initiated WHPP may be able to reach workers not typically reached by employer-based programs, barriers still seem to exist for workers with less secure contracts, poor work-life balance, and fewer health complaints.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
