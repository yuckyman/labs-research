# Sampling suggestions for bedload sediment transport measurements near the threshold of motion

## Abstract

<jats:p>In gravel-bed rivers, sediment transport is commonly intermittent, with low background rates of motion punctuated by short, rare events of elevated activity. Although the physical mechanisms explaining this transport behaviour remain debated, its implications for sediment-transport measurements need to be considered when determining sampling strategy. Here we established a consistent, data-driven framework to quantify convergence metrics, the time and number of samples needed to obtain a representative estimate of average sediment transport. Using Monte Carlo based bootstrap analysis, we quantified the relationship between sampling duration and variability in mean sediment transport estimates. Convergence time varied by up to seven orders of magnitude across experiments, while the number of samples required for convergence varied by only one order of magnitude, showing the importance of sampling frequency. Sampling frequency and transport stage (the ratio of Shields number to critical Shields number) were the dominant controls on convergence metrics. We derived an empirical equation for estimating the number of samples required for convergence, applicable across a wide range of experimental transport conditions and sources of variability.</jats:p>
