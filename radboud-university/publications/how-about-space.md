# How About Space?

## Abstract

<jats:p>This chapter presents a spatial approach as a complement to common explanations for the creation and development of regional organizations, a perspective that is comparatively underrepresented in political science research. Particularly with regard to the analytical significance of nation states and the separation of nation states and regional/global systems, the spatial approach aims to provide an alternative to the prevailing approaches in regionalism research. The chapter first provides a critical discussion of the understanding of space in classical theories of International Relations (IR). It then presents a perspective from critical geography, according to which space encompasses more than territory. Based on constructivist approaches, critical geopolitics argues that geographical units are social or discursive constructions of space, and it is this construction that is the focus of investigation. In addition, a network approach is presented that seeks to integrate the functional logic of traditional IR theories with a spatial logic. Examples from ASEAN and SADC illustrate the argument.</jats:p>
