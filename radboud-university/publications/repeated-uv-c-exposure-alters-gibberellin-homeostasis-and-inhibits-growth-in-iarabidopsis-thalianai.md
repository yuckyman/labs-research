# Repeated UV-C exposure alters gibberellin homeostasis and inhibits growth in
                  <i>Arabidopsis thaliana</i>

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                <jats:p>
                  Ultraviolet-C (UV-C) radiation can be highly damaging to plants, yet its effects on gibberellin (GA) homeostasis are not well understood. In this study, we show that short daily UV-C pulse treatments (12 s, 1,200 J m
                  <jats:sup>-2</jats:sup>
                  ) applied for seven days reduce plant height and delay flowering in
                  <jats:italic>Arabidopsis thaliana</jats:italic>
                  . Endogenous levels of the GA biosynthesis precursors GA
                  <jats:sub>12</jats:sub>
                  , GA
                  <jats:sub>53</jats:sub>
                  , GA
                  <jats:sub>15</jats:sub>
                  , and GA
                  <jats:sub>24</jats:sub>
                  , the bioactive GA
                  <jats:sub>4</jats:sub>
                  , and the GA catabolites GA
                  <jats:sub>34</jats:sub>
                  and GA
                  <jats:sub>110</jats:sub>
                  are all lower in UV-C treated plants than in untreated controls. These changes were accompanied by lower transcript levels of the GA biosynthesis genes
                  <jats:italic>KS, GA13ox1, GA20ox1</jats:italic>
                  , and
                  <jats:italic>GA3ox1</jats:italic>
                  , together with opposing changes in the expression of
                  <jats:italic>GA2ox</jats:italic>
                  genes. Exogenous GA
                  <jats:sub>4</jats:sub>
                  restores growth in UV-C-treated plants, suggesting that reduced GA availability contributes to UV-C-induced growth inhibition. Consistent with this finding, the GA-signalling mutant
                  <jats:italic>gdella</jats:italic>
                  and the GA-biosynthesis mutants
                  <jats:italic>kao1</jats:italic>
                  and
                  <jats:italic>kao2</jats:italic>
                  show strongly reduced UV-C responses. Together, these findings highlight the importance of GA metabolism and signalling in the developmental response to repeated UV-C exposure, and suggest that exposure regimen influences the dynamics of UV-C-induced hormonal responses.
                </jats:p>
