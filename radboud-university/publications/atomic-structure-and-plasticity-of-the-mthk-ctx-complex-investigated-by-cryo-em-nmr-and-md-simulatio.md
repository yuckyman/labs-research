# Atomic structure and plasticity of the MthK-CTX complex investigated by cryo-EM, NMR, and MD simulations

## Abstract

<jats:p>Scorpion toxins can block potassium channels, thereby disrupting cellular excitability and causing symptoms such as pain, muscle spasms, or paralysis. Here, we use an integrated structural biology approach to reveal the binding mode of the scorpion toxin charybdotoxin (CTX) to the MthK ion channel, a model system for human large-conductance potassium (BK) channels. Cryo-EM reveals the overall architecture of the MthK-CTX complex and highlights key interaction sites. Complementary solution and solid-state NMR experiments on the toxin, ion channel, and the complex detect chemical perturbations in both partners and show pronounced effects on the selectivity filter residues of MthK upon toxin binding. Using ammonium ions as NMR-visible probes, we could directly demonstrate how toxin binding leads to a change of ion configuration in the MthK selectivity filter. Molecular dynamics simulations provide additional insight into the
energetics and conformational variability of the complex. Together, these results support a mechanism in which CTX acts as a pore-blocking ′master key′, anchoring via its conserved lysine side-chain while retaining sufficient mobility to bind tightly across related potassium channels.</jats:p>
