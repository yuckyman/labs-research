# Variant curation of the largest compendium of
                  <i>FOXL2</i>
                  coding and non-coding sequence and structural variants in BPES

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                <jats:p>
                  Heterozygous
                  <jats:italic>FOXL2</jats:italic>
                  (non-)coding sequence and structural variants (SVs) lead to blepharophimosis, ptosis and epicanthus inversus syndrome (BPES), a rare, autosomal dominant developmental disorder characterized by a completely penetrant eyelid malformation and incompletely penetrant primary ovarian insufficiency (POI).
                </jats:p>
                <jats:p>We collected variants from our in-house database, generated via clinical genetic testing and downstream research testing in the Center for Medical Genetics Ghent, Belgium (2001-2024), and via literature and other resources in the same period. All retrieved variants were categorized using ACMG/AMP classifications to increase the knowledge of pathogenicity.</jats:p>
                <jats:p>
                  We collected 413 unique genetic defects of the
                  <jats:italic>FOXL2</jats:italic>
                  region, including 76 novel variants, in 864 index patients. Of these, 87% of patients were identified with a coding
                  <jats:italic>FOXL2</jats:italic>
                  sequence variant. The polyalanine tract is a known mutational hotspot of
                  <jats:italic>FOXL2</jats:italic>
                  , illustrated here by the high percentage of pathogenic polyalanine expansions (24%). Furthermore, the molecular spectrum in typical BPES index patients is characterized by 8% coding deletions and 3% deletions located up- and downstream of
                  <jats:italic>FOXL2</jats:italic>
                  . The remaining 2% carry translocations along with chromosomal rearrangements of 3q23.
                </jats:p>
                <jats:p>
                  This uniform and structured reclassification, incorporating the largest dataset of variants implicated in
                  <jats:italic>FOXL2</jats:italic>
                  -associated disease so far, will improve both the diagnosis as well as genetic counselling for individuals with BPES.
                </jats:p>
