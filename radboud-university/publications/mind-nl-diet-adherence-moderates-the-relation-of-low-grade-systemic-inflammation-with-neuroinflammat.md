# MIND-NL diet adherence moderates the relation of low-grade systemic inflammation with neuroinflammation and cognitive functioning: an exploratory cross-sectional study in older adults

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                <jats:sec>
                  <jats:title>Background</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>Observational studies have linked high adherence to the “Mediterranean-Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay” (MIND) diet to improved cognitive functions in older adults. In addition, we examined the role of intestinal barrier permeability in MIND diet associations with (neuro)inflammation.</jats:p>
                </jats:sec>
                <jats:sec>
                  <jats:title>Methods</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>
                    We included 88 older adults (60-75 year) at risk of cognitive decline. MIND-NL diet adherence was assessed using a food frequency questionnaire. Neuroinflammation-associated metabolites (myo-inositol, choline and creatine) were measured in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex with proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (
                    <jats:sup>1</jats:sup>
                    H-MRS). Cognitive functioning was assessed with a neuropsychological test battery.
                  </jats:p>
                </jats:sec>
                <jats:sec>
                  <jats:title>Results</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>
                    Linear models showed that both MIND diet adherence and systemic inflammation did not predict neuroinflammation or cognition independently. However, MIND diet adherence significantly moderated the relation between systemic inflammation and neuroinflammation (β=-0.11,
                    <jats:italic>p</jats:italic>
                    =0.04) as well as between systemic inflammation and cognition (β=0.044,
                    <jats:italic>p</jats:italic>
                    =0.02).                     =0.05). Finally, within participants with lower MIND adherence (median split at ≤8.75), systemic inflammation mediated the relation between the intestinal barrier permeability and neuroinflammation (β=0.427 [0.072; 0.891],
                    <jats:italic>p</jats:italic>
                    =0.04).
                  </jats:p>
