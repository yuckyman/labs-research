# The Selenocysteine tRNA Saga: Gatekeeper of the Eukaryotic Selenoproteome in Health and Disease

## Abstract

<jats:p>Selenium (Se) is an essential trace element whose biological activity is mediated through selenoproteins – a family of ~ 25 human proteins containing the rare amino acid selenocysteine (Sec) at their active site. The biosynthesis and incorporation of Sec depend entirely on a single, structurally unique transfer RNA: the selenocysteine tRNA (tRNA). This molecule acts as the molecular bottleneck of the entire selenoproteome, integrating the cellular selenium signal and controlling selenoprotein synthesis through a tightly regulated recoding mechanism. This chapter traces the saga of tRNA, from its discovery as an anomalous suppressor tRNA in the 1970s–1980s to its recognition as the carrier of the 21st genetically encoded amino acid and a master regulator of selenoprotein expression. We describe its unique molecular architecture and the three-step biosynthetic pathway by which serine is converted into Sec on the tRNA scaffold. We detail the UGA recoding machinery – selenocysteine insertion sequence (SECIS) elements, SECISBP2, and the eukaryotic elongation factor for Sec (EEFSEC) – and connect this molecular biology to two routes to human disease: selenium deficiency resulting from lower intake, which collapses selenoprotein expression, and rare inborn errors of the recoding machinery. An integrative framework links Se bioavailability to selenoprotein-dependent phenotypes, including redox homeostasis, thyroid hormone metabolism, immune function, and ferroptosis resistance.</jats:p>
