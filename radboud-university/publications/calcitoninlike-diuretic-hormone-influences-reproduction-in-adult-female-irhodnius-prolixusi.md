                  <jats:p>
                    Calcitonin‐like diuretic hormone (CT/DH) is a pleiotropic neuropeptide in insects that plays a role in regulating diuresis in
                    <jats:italic>Rhodnius prolixus</jats:italic>
                    , a blood‐feeding insect and a vector of Chagas’ disease. Here, we examine the role of this neuropeptide in another feeding‐dependent event, that of reproduction in female
                    <jats:italic>R. prolixus</jats:italic>

                    <jats:italic>Rhopr‐CT/DH‐R‐1B/C</jats:italic>
                    and
                    <jats:italic>Rhopr‐CT/DH‐R‐2B</jats:italic>
                    are present in reproductive tissues and fat body, and appear to be regulated post blood meal, a stimulus that triggers diuresis and reproduction. Knockdown of the two receptors using RNA interference reduces the number of eggs produced and their hatching rate. Downregulation of
                    <jats:italic>Rhopr‐CT/DH‐Rs</jats:italic>

                    <jats:italic>RhoprVg1</jats:italic>
                    in the fat body. Knockdown does lead to a decrease in transcript expression of
                    <jats:italic>RhoprVg1</jats:italic>

                    <jats:italic>RhoprVgR</jats:italic>
