# 360° video presentations, professional vision, and cognitive-affective experience in physical education teacher education

## Abstract

<jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Introduction</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Using videos to develop professional vision is a commonly used tool. In recent years, 360° videos have gained increasing popularity, and their impact on knowledge acquisition has been the subject of growing research interest. Therefore, this study investigates how different 360° video presentation formats support the development of professional vision and cognitive-affective processes in prospective physical education (PE) teachers.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Method</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Across three conditions—fixed screen (low immersion), interactive screen (moderate immersion), and head-mounted display (HMD; high immersion) - 123 PE master’s students observed authentic 360° PE classroom scenarios and completed open noticing tasks as well as questionnaires on interest, motivation, cognitive capacity, self-efficacy, and embodiment.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Results</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>
                      A MANOVA and subsequent
                      <jats:italic>post-hoc</jats:italic>
                      tests showed that participants in the HMD condition produced substantially longer noticing responses (as proxy for engagement) than those in the screen conditions, although the number of classroom events noticed did not differ between formats, suggesting more elaborated - but not necessarily more numerous - perceptions under high technical immersion. High immersion further led to higher interest, pleasure, mental effort, and embodiment (possible actions, self-location), whereas no effects emerged for mental load or teaching-related self-efficacy. Mediation analyses using structural equation modeling revealed a significant indirect effect of video presentation on response length via self-location, indicating that feeling physically present in the virtual gym mediated the positive impact of high immersion on professional vision, while other cognitive-affective factors did not show significant mediation effects.
                    </jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Discussion</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>The findings extend the Cognitive Affective Model of Immersive Learning (CAMIL) to PE teacher education by demonstrating that technically immersive 360° video—especially via HMD—enhances motivational and embodiment-related experiences and deepens verbalized noticing, but does not automatically increase the quantity of detected events, highlighting both the potential and the limits of immersive video for fostering professional vision in complex PE settings.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
