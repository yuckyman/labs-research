# Bellmunt Risk Score as a Prognostic Tool in Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer Survival

## Abstract

<jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-4">
                    <jats:title>Importance</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) is an incurable disease with a highly variable clinical course, necessitating robust prognostic tools to guide individualized patient management.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-5">
                    <jats:title>Objective</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>To validate the prognostic ability of the Bellmunt Risk Score (BRS) for predicting survival in patients with mCRPC.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-6">
                    <jats:title>Design, Setting, and Participants</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>For this prognostic study, a post hoc analysis was conducted of 2 international, multicenter, phase 3 randomized clinical trials of men (aged ≥18 years) with mCRPC (ACIS [first-line setting] and ELM-PC-5 [postdocetaxel setting]). The ACIS trial enrolled patients between December 2014 and August 2016. The ELM-PC-5 trial enrolled patients between October 2010 and February 2013. Data analyses were conducted between September 2024 and March 2025.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-7">
                    <jats:title>Exposure</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Baseline BRS (range, 0-3), calculated by assigning 1 point for each of 3 factors (Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status score ≥1, hemoglobin level &amp;amp;lt;10 g/dL, and liver metastases).</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-8">
                    <jats:title>Main Outcomes and Measures</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>The primary outcomes, overall survival (OS) and radiographic progression-free survival, were assessed via Kaplan-Meier and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analysis.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-9">
                    <jats:title>Results</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>This analysis included 678 evaluable male participants from the ACIS trial (median age, 71 years [range, 48-92 years]) and 1078 from the ELM-PC-5 trial (median age, 70 years [range, 43-89 years]), for a total of 1756 participants. In the ELM-PC-5 trial, the median OS decreased from 23.0 months (95% CI, 21.50 months to not reached) for patients with a BRS of 0 to 3.2 months (95% CI, 1.4-8.6 months) for those with a BRS of 3. AHRs for OS were 1.65 (95% CI, 1.31-2.07) for patients with a BRS of 1, 2.93 (95% CI, 2.22-3.87) for those with a BRS of 2, and 4.43 (95% CI, 2.65-7.40) for those with a BRS of 3 compared with those with a BRS of 0. The BRS remained a robust and independent prognostic factor for both outcomes in all multivariable models.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec id="ab-zoi260025-10">
                    <jats:title>Conclusions and Relevance</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>In this prognostic study of patients with mCRPC, the BRS was validated as a prognostic tool that provides clinically meaningful information across different treatment lines. Due to its simplicity, the BRS is a practical aid for guiding treatment decisions and facilitating prognostic discussions in routine clinical care.</jats:p>
