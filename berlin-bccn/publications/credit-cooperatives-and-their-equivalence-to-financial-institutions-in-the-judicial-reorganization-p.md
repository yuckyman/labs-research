# CREDIT COOPERATIVES AND THEIR EQUIVALENCE TO FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS IN THE JUDICIAL REORGANIZATION PROCESS

## Abstract

<jats:p>This article analyzes the legal treatment given to credits held by credit cooperatives in judicial reorganization proceedings, from the perspective of the functional equivalence of these entities to financial institutions. Initially, the study explores the historical and normative context of cooperativism, distinguishing the classic cooperative act from the activity carried out by credit cooperatives in the contemporary financial market. The study addresses the controversy surrounding the nature of these credits, contrasting the principle of promoting cooperativism with the need for legal certainty and stability of the National Financial System. Through a doctrinal and jurisprudential analysis, it seeks to demonstrate that the operational and regulatory similarity between credit cooperatives and banks justifies the application of equivalent bankruptcy effects.</jats:p>
