# Abstract C061: Addressing unmet non-medical needs for patients with cancer: Preliminary results from the ConnectedNest Trial

## Abstract

<jats:title>Abstract</jats:title>
                  <jats:p>Approximately half of the 8 million cancer survivors in the United States experience non-medical factors, including financial hardship, housing instability, food insecurity, and transportation barriers. These non-medical factors are associated with delayed care, reduced treatment adherence, increased healthcare utilization, and poorer survivorship outcomes. Despite the recognized importance of addressing these needs, healthcare systems often lack scalable approaches to identify patients and connect them with appropriate community resources.To address this gap, we developed ConnectedNest, a digital social care referral platform with multiple integrated interfaces. The patient interface allows them to complete social needs screenings and connect directly to community resources; the community web portal allows community-based organizations to register, manage, and track their services. We deployed ConnectedNest in two medium-sized healthcare systems in Minnesota. This randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT06559228) evaluates the effectiveness of ConnectedNest compared with usual care.A total of 400 adults with a cancer diagnosis were enrolled and randomized to either usual care or the ConnectedNest intervention. Participants assigned to the intervention were asked to download and use the ConnectedNest mobile application. All participants completed a set of assessments at baseline to understand their non-medical social needs and demographics. Participants were directed to complete additional surveys at 30, 60, and 90 days to collect patient-reported outcomes on the following: number of self-reported needs, needs addressed, quality of life, and satisfaction of care.Preliminary results of the study show that of the 400 patients enrolled, 323 completed both the baseline and demographic surveys. The patient population was approximately 93% White, with 3.2% identifying as African American and the remaining identifying as Other. Patients self-reported over 10 different cancer diagnoses. The largest populations were Breast (25%) and Gynecological (20%), followed by Colorectal and Lung at 6.9% and 5.2%. Over three-quarters of the patients (76%) were on active treatment. Final analyses will be completed following trial completion in August 2026.</jats:p>
