# What Shapes Well-Being in Secondary School Students? Findings from a Lagged Exposure-Wide Approach

## Abstract

<p>Students’ school well-being is pivotal to both immediate academic success and long-term life outcomes, yet research has rarely compared a broad range of potential predictors within a common longitudinal framework. In this preregistered study, we used a lagged exposure-wide approach to examine 55 theoretically and empirically selected candidate factors in three annual waves of data from 2,401 German students (MAgeT1=10.75 years, 47% female), assessed from Grade 5 onward. Individual characteristics and students’ perceptions of teachers and teaching quality were associated with changes in school well-being. Notably, these associations were observed beyond academic performance, parental characteristics, and a broad range of covariates. Different factors were associated with school belonging and school satisfaction, highlighting the multifaceted nature of school well-being. By systematically comparing a broad set of candidate factors, our findings identify modifiable factors that may represent promising targets for future interventions aiming to support students’ well-being alongside their academic development.</p>
