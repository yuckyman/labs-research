# Beyond Dominance and Restraint

## Abstract

<jats:p>This chapter advances Critical Astropolitics as an integrative framework for analyzing security, power, and inequality in outer space. Dominant astropolitical approaches both realist and liberal have centered deterrence, dominance, and strategic stability, often treating security as self-evident and space as a neutral extension of terrestrial geopolitics. Drawing on Critical Security Studies, securitization theory, Black feminist thought, and critical geopolitics, this chapter challenges that assumption by conceptualizing space security as a socially constructed practice shaped by discourse, institutional authority, and worldview. The chapter contributes to theory by reframing astropolitics as a site of constitutive power where sovereignty, authority, and future political orders are actively produced. For policymakers, it offers a diagnostic lens for evaluating the sustainability and legitimacy of contemporary space security practices as humanity's engagement with space deepens.</jats:p>
