# Value and usefulness of theory in teaching and teacher education: perspectives of teacher educators

## Abstract

<jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Introduction</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>This study examines how teacher educators at universities, teacher seminars, and schools conceptualize and use theory - defined broadly as scientific professional knowledge, including theoretical frameworks, empirical research findings, and evidence-informed orientation - in their mentoring and teaching practices.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Method</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>Using a praxeological-sociological approach and documentary method analyses of interview data, we reconstruct the implicit beliefs that guide educators' actions when mentoring pre-service teachers.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Results</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>The findings reveal four variations of theory-conceptions, differing markedly in how theory is conceived of, valued, and incorporated in mentoring.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
                  <jats:sec>
                    <jats:title>Discussion</jats:title>
                    <jats:p>These divergences show inconsistencies across institutional sites of teacher education and may shape the extent to which pre-service teachers can develop theory-informed professional competence. The study highlights the need for targeted professional development for teacher educators and stronger program-level alignment to better integrate theory and practice in practical placements.</jats:p>
                  </jats:sec>
